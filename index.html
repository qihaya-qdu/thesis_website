In this demonstration, the LLM acts as the decision-making component of the game-playing agent. It generates subgoals and selects the next target based on the current map, position and available keys. The program checks target reachability using BFS before executing the corresponding movements.

Abstract

Long-horizon decision making by large language model (LLM) agents becomes especially difficult in tasks with irreversible resource constraints, where locally reasonable actions may still be globally harmful. To study this problem, a simplified Mota-style dungeon environment is constructed in which keys, doors, floor transitions, and final objectives create long-term dependencies. A series of LLM-based agents is then designed and compared, progressing from local action and route scoring to subgoal-chain reasoning, programmatic reachability verification, and reflection. Early approaches frequently suffered from goal drift and poor resource awareness. To address these limitations, LLM-generated subgoals are combined with BFS-based reachability verification, allowing intermediate decisions to be checked against the final objective before execution. An advanced version also includes reset and reflection mechanisms that record failure experiences as reusable lessons. The results show that explicit subgoal chains and programmatic verification improve decision consistency and reliability in long-horizon, resource-constrained tasks. More importantly, the study proposes and analyses a long-horizon decision-making framework that combines autonomous LLM-generated subgoal reasoning with deterministic, BFS-based reachability verification. Through an evolutionary sequence of agent versions, it reveals the role of subgoal chains in mitigating goal drift, preserving the salience of intermediate objectives, and improving success in resource-constrained tasks. Overall, the study provides experimental evidence and methodological guidance for building more reliable long-horizon LLM agents.
